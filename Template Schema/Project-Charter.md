## Project Charter

>A project charter is a concise, formal document that officially authorizes a project’s existence and grants the project manager the authority to apply organizational resources to project activities. 


---

1. General Project Information
1. Project Purpose & Objectives
1. Project Scope Boundaries
1. High level timelines and Milestones
1. Financial & Resource Estimates
1. Risks, Assumptions and Constraints
1. Key Stakeholders and Governance Roles
1. Formal Approvals

---

>Brief Explanation of sections

```
1. General Project Information
• Project Name: A clear, descriptive title that communicates the core focus.
• Project Sponsor: The executive or stakeholder providing financial backing and authority.
• Project Manager: The individual named to lead execution, including their designated authority limits.
• Creation/Revision Date: The current version date to ensure team and stakeholder alignment. 
```

```
2. Project Purpose & Objectives
• Business Case / Need: The underlying problem, business opportunity, or strategic alignment driving the investment.
• SMART Objectives: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound target outcomes.
• Success Criteria: Explicit metrics and standards used to evaluate whether goals have been successfully met. 
```

```
3. Project Scope Boundaries
• In-Scope Elements: The core deliverables, features, and boundaries of work to be completed.
• Out-of-Scope Elements: Explicitly stated exclusions to protect the team from scope creep. 
```

```
4. High-Level Timeline & Milestones
• Key Milestones: Crucial checkpoints or dates, such as gathering requirements, architecture sign-offs, development phases, and final rollout.
• Estimated Schedule: A high-level view of the target launch or phase transitions, noting key dependencies. 
```

```
5. Financial & Resource Estimates
• Budget Overview: A preliminary cost estimate, often allowing for a flexible variance percentage early in the lifecycle.
• Resource Requirements: Critical assets, technology platforms, equipment, or cross-departmental commitments needed. 
```

```
6. Risks, Assumptions, & Constraints
• High-Level Risks: Known threats or roadblocks that could delay the project or increase costs, alongside initial mitigation ideas.
• Assumptions: Factors taken as true, real, or certain without proof for planning purposes.
• Constraints: Defined limits on time, cost, staffing, technical standards, or regulatory parameters. 
```

```
7. Key Stakeholders & Governance Roles
• Stakeholder Register: A listing of key clients, operational users, and core decision-makers.
• Roles & Responsibilities: Clarification of who handles day-to-day execution versus who approves changes. 
```

```
8. Formal Approvals
• Sign-Off Block: Designated lines for signature and date from the executive sponsor or steering committee to formally trigger project kickoff. 
```
