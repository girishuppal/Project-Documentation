## Business Case
---

`A standard business case outline provides a structured justification for a project, investment, or initiative by proving its value, feasibility, and alignment with organizational goals.`

---
1. Executive Summary
1. Problem or Opportunity Statement
1. Strategic Context and Alignment
1. Analysis of Options
1. Financial Analysis and Investment Appraisal
1. Risk Assessment & Mitigation
1. Implementation Plan & Timescales
1. Governance & Evaluation

---

`Detailed descriptin of each section`

```
Executive Summary
• Overview: A high-level summary of the project, the core problem, the proposed solution, and the expected return on investment (ROI). (Tip: Write this section last).
• Key Recommendation: A clear statement of what is being requested (budget, resources, or approval) and the anticipated timeline.
```

```
Problem or Opportunity Statement
• The Issue: A specific description of the internal/external pain point or missed market opportunity.
• Evidence & Scale: Data, metrics, or financial impacts showing how much the problem currently costs the organization.
• Root Cause: Analysis of why the problem exists and what happens if no action is taken (the "do-nothing" baseline).
```
```
Strategic Context and Alignment
• Organizational Goals: How the project supports the company's broader mission, strategic plan, or government/board priorities.
• Stakeholders: Identification of who is affected, who sponsors the initiative, and who will govern execution.
```
```
Analysis of Options
• Alternatives Considered: A comparison of possible approaches, typically categorized as:
	• Do nothing / status quo
	• Do the minimum (incremental fix)
	• Do something alternative (alternative project)
	• The preferred solution
• Selection Justification: Why the chosen option delivers the best balance of cost, speed, and benefit compared to the alternatives.
```
```
Financial Analysis and Investment Appraisal
• Project Costs: One-time capital expenses, setup fees, software, or labor needed to build/launch.
• Operational Costs: Ongoing maintenance, licensing, or support expenses.
• Expected Benefits: Quantifiable financial gains (revenue increases, cost savings) and qualitative improvements (employee morale, customer satisfaction).
• ROI & Metrics: Net present value (NPV), payback period, or break-even timing.
```
```
Risk Assessment and Mitigation
• Identified Risks: Operational, financial, technical, or market risks associated with doing the project (and risks of not doing it).
• Mitigation Strategies: Action plans, controls, or contingency reserves for each major risk.
```
```
Implementation Plan and Timescales
• Project Timeline: Key phases, milestones, and estimated completion dates.
• Resource Requirements: Staffing, hardware, software, and cross-functional support needed.
• Benefits Realization: A schedule showing when and how the projected benefits will actually be tracked and delivered.
```
```
Governance and Evaluation
• Project Management: Who leads the project, how progress is reported, and key performance indicators (KPIs) to measure success.
• Review Process: How the business case will be monitored as a living document throughout execution.
```
